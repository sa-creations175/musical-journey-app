Every button here re-lights the keyboard; Hear it plays. Thickness is a ladder, each rung adding a note, and the chord names underneath change to match what is sounding. Bass only: just the root notes, low (C G A F). Triads: root in the bass, the plain triad in the hand (C G Am F). Guide tones: the seventh-chord names (Cmaj7 G7 Am7 Fmaj7), only the 3 and 7 in the hand. Seventh chords: same names, 3 5 7 in the hand. Full voicing: the ninth names (Cmaj9 G9 Am9 Fmaj9), 3 5 7 9 in the hand. The last three are the words the Shapes & Patterns 2 5 1 drill already uses. Then a different starting point, the dominant variation, and the bass line: one button per move, up or down, so you can hear the 2 fall to the 5 and climb to the 1 as well as climb both times. This is the same player the ear training side will use.

Set the root by tapping a key on the keyboard or a letter in the row (either one, they mirror each other), then a quality; the chord sits in the highlighted slot and the keyboard shows it voiced. Taps are silent; Hear chord plays the slot you are on, Hear all plays your chords in order once every slot is filled. Submit only grades; nothing plays unless you tap a Hear button. Change your mind as often as you like; nothing moves on until you tap Next chord or another slot. Clear chord empties only the slot you are on. Keys are coloured by each note's job in the chord. The bass note carries a green band along the foot of its key, so if the bass line climbs onto a key the hand is using you see both. Root deep green, 3rd light green (minor 3rd teal), 5th grey, 7th amber (maj7 pale amber), 9th pink, the app's own chord-tone colours. Before you submit, the keyboard shows exactly the chord you chose, nothing added: pick min7 and you see C E♭ G B♭. Voicing only changes how it is laid out. One hand: the chord in the hand, you pick the inversion. Both hands: root in the bass, the chord in the hand. Rootless: root in the bass, the hand plays the chord minus its root, A form or B form (greyed out on a triad, which has no 7 to build on). Chords after the first are voiced to move as little as possible from the one before. Tapping any octave of the keyboard sets the root. Three slots for a 2 5 1, four for a 1 5 6 4. This is the slash chord picker plus a quality row, so it is a sibling of that picker, not the same one.

No letter row on this card: the keyboard is the answer. Tap a key to choose it, tap again to remove it. Octave does not matter; E♭ in either octave counts once. Taps are silent; Submit only grades, and the Play it panel has the Hear it button. Same shell for the minor card ("In C minor pentatonic, the notes are _____").

Key Signatures & Relationships

The relative minor of the key of A♭ major is _____

Its root
choose a root

Letter row or keyboard, either sets the root (tap A♭ on the keys instead of A then ♭); the tap is silent and lights only that note. On a correct Submit the whole natural minor scale lights, root in green, because the card is about the key. The reverse card ("The relative major of F minor is _____") lights the major scale.

A single low tonic note to orient (the same rule as the progression card), then the slash chord where it lives. Each shape carries its own contexts, written once for the shape: inversions (1/3, 5/7) walk, the sus (4/5) resolves, the cadential (1/5) leads to the 5, the pedals (5/1, 2m/1) sit over a held 1, and 1/4 settles home. The chords around the slash chord are voice-led to and from it; your hand shape is the anchor.

Two steps, top then bottom, like a fraction. Top chord: a letter in the row or a key in the upper two octaves; quality starts on major, so the chord lights in the hand at once and you only touch the quality row for a minor or seventh chord. Bottom note: its own letter row appears once the top chord is set, or tap a key in the low octave (that octave is the bass, as on the progression card); it carries the green band. Hand shape is the inversion the right hand plays (3rd inversion greyed on a triad); root position by default, since the bottom note does the talking (1/3 is the 1 in first inversion either way). Grading: chord root, chord type, bottom note. Typing "G/B" is gone.

Sound on the progression card: a single low tonic note to orient, then the chords in order (Silas, 9 Sep: the home chord in front was confusing). The scale cards still play the key's home chord first, then the scale as a run, per the 8 Sep rule.
